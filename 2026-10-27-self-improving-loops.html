Can my bots get better just by reading their own logs?

2026-10-27

I bookmarked a short post from @kunchenguid last night that claims "you can make almost any program self-improve." The recipe is three steps.

First, ask your agent to "help me plan comprehensive logging such that we can replay what the user experienced, meaningful internal operations, and visible outcomes." Second, set up a daily loop with this prompt: "analyze my logs from this program in the past 24h thoroughly, identify broken behaviors, user friction, inefficiencies, and propose improvements sorted by estimated impact." Third, "watch your code get better every day."

It sounds almost too simple. But I think it's pointing at something real, and it made me look at my own bots differently.

Feedback is the thing agents are hungry for

The same morning I saved a post from @poteto summing up a talk by Yaron Minsky on making codebases better for agents. The first bullet is "agents thrive on feedback, and types are one of the best kinds." Another one is about expect tests: "just print stuff out and capture it in the test. makes behavior legible."

Put the two posts side by side and they're saying the same thing at different sizes. A type checker gives an agent feedback in seconds. A log of what actually happened yesterday gives it feedback in a day. Either way, the agent stops guessing about how the thing behaves and starts reading it.

The part I like in the logging recipe is the word "replay." Not just errors, but what the user experienced. Most of my logs, when I have any, only say that something failed. They don't say what I was trying to do, or what I saw on my end.

My bots already run on a loop

I have a handful of bots that run every morning on a timer. This blog is one of them. They do their job and then mostly vanish until the next day.

What they don't do is look back. If one of them gets something slightly wrong every day, nothing notices except me, and only if I happen to read closely. The fix usually happens when I get annoyed enough to say something in chat.

So the gap isn't the loop. I already have loops. The gap is that the loop only does the work, and never reviews the work.

What I'd want to try

The obvious version is a second daily job that reads what the first one did and suggests fixes. But "propose improvements sorted by estimated impact" is where I'd be careful. An agent reviewing its own logs will always find something to improve, the same way a person asked to find problems always finds some. If every suggestion gets applied, I'd expect the bot to get busier rather than better.

So I think the useful version has two rules. The logs have to capture what I saw, not just what the bot did. And the review has to suggest, not change things, so I still decide what's worth fixing.

I'm curious whether a week of that would surface anything I don't already know. My guess is the first few days will be noise, and then one boring pattern will show up that I've been quietly working around for weeks.
